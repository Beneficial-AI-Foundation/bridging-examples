## 1. Jasmin: from random-byte calls to security statements

The developments below connect implementations to cryptographic models through explicit coins, interpretations of RNG requests, or distributions on initial state. For each, the relevant questions are where probability enters and which correspondence is proved.

**The Jasmin/EasyCrypt interface.** Jasmin represents [`#randombytes`](https://github.com/jasmin-lang/jasmin/blob/1a54c9292f4de8003a2d3c42ebf51c674ea6ecea/proofs/lang/syscall.v) by an external stateful operation. In `jasmin2ec`, [`ec_randombytes`](https://github.com/jasmin-lang/jasmin/blob/1a54c9292f4de8003a2d3c42ebf51c674ea6ecea/compiler/src/toEC.ml#L2010-L2044) emits both a [`Syscall_t`](https://github.com/jasmin-lang/jasmin/blob/1a54c9292f4de8003a2d3c42ebf51c674ea6ecea/compiler/src/toEC.ml#L2010-L2044) interface and a default [`Syscall`](https://github.com/jasmin-lang/jasmin/blob/1a54c9292f4de8003a2d3c42ebf51c674ea6ecea/compiler/src/toEC.ml#L2010-L2044) module that samples uniform byte arrays. The extracted client [`M(SC)`](https://github.com/jasmin-lang/jasmin/blob/1a54c9292f4de8003a2d3c42ebf51c674ea6ecea/compiler/src/toEC.ml#L2072-L2073) is parameterised by the provider. Instantiating it with [`Syscall`](https://github.com/jasmin-lang/jasmin/blob/1a54c9292f4de8003a2d3c42ebf51c674ea6ecea/compiler/src/toEC.ml#L2010-L2044) supplies ideal probabilistic semantics to source RNG calls. The executable instead calls [`__jasmin_syscall_randombytes__`](https://github.com/jasmin-lang/jasmin/blob/1a54c9292f4de8003a2d3c42ebf51c674ea6ecea/compiler/syscall/jasmin_syscall.c), whose linked implementation invokes OS facilities. Agreement between that provider and the ideal handler is a separate assumption. [[1]][r1]

**Formosa ML-KEM.** The Libjade API accepts explicit coins. In [`correctness768.ec`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/avx2/sct/correctness/768/correctness768.ec#L116-L171), [`keygen_correct`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/avx2/sct/correctness/768/correctness768.ec#L116-L133), [`encaps_correct`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/avx2/sct/correctness/768/correctness768.ec#L135-L151), and [`decaps_correct`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/avx2/sct/correctness/768/correctness768.ec#L153-L171) relate extracted Jasmin to the algorithmic ML-KEM specification, matching representations, supplied coins, and return codes. In [`MLKEMSecurity.ec`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec), [`kg_bridge`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec#L699-L700), [`enc_bridge`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec#L702-L707), and [`dec_bridge`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec#L709-L715) connect that specification to [`MLKEM_Op(DummyRO)`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec#L693-L715); encapsulation and decapsulation project away validation flags. Here [`DummyRO`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec#L693-L697) computes a concrete hash. The later [`keygen_eq`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec#L956-L987), [`enc_eq`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec#L989-L1006), and [`dec_eq`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec#L1009-L1037) relate the random-oracle model to the abstract Fujisaki-Okamoto construction. [`mlkem768_secure`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/Security768.ec#L54-L148) states the resulting specification-level IND-CCA bound. Probability enters through [`MLKEM_Op.kg`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec#L620-L630) and [`.enc`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/proofs/mlkem/spec/properties/MLKEMSecurity.ec#L650-L659): they sample coins from `srand`, then call deterministic `*_derand` procedures. Thus the code correspondence and the caller's RNG contract remain separate. Current assurance metadata labels this [`functional`](https://github.com/formosa-crypto/formosa-libjade/blob/90ba5dc4d9da2b6950589c58a52282caa1dbfdfd/docs/assurance.md): correctness and specification security are proved separately, without a consolidated implementation-security reduction. [[2]][r2]

**Leakage-Free Probabilistic Jasmin Programs (CPP 2025).** The source rejection sampler calls [`#randombytes`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-leakage-freeness/blob/e788f8aa99eaffc40e6dcce0ca9331814aed7613/src/bn_rsample.jazz); the extracted client is instantiated with the uniform [`Syscall`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-leakage-freeness/blob/e788f8aa99eaffc40e6dcce0ca9331814aed7613/proof/jasmin_extracts/W64_RejectionSamplingExtract.ec#L27-L46) handler. In [`UniformSampling_Concrete.ec`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-leakage-freeness/blob/e788f8aa99eaffc40e6dcce0ca9331814aed7613/proof/rejection_sampling/UniformSampling_Concrete.ec#L495-L526), [`rsample_aspec`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-leakage-freeness/blob/e788f8aa99eaffc40e6dcce0ca9331814aed7613/proof/rejection_sampling/UniformSampling_Concrete.ec#L495-L515) relates the extracted sampler to `ASpecFp.rsample`, and [`bn_rsample_pmf`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-leakage-freeness/blob/e788f8aa99eaffc40e6dcce0ca9331814aed7613/proof/rejection_sampling/UniformSampling_Concrete.ec#L518-L526) proves its exact point probabilities after decoding machine words as integers. The bytes' law is supplied by the handler; uniformity of the accepted result follows by proof. The separate theorem [`bn_rsample1_LF`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-leakage-freeness/blob/e788f8aa99eaffc40e6dcce0ca9331814aed7613/proof/rejection_sampling/leakage_freeness_prhl/UniformSampling_Concrete_LF.ec#L136-L159) establishes leakage-freeness for a sampler with variable running time. [[3]][r3]

**Schnorr in Jasmin.** [`W64_SchnorrProtocol.ec`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-zk/blob/dd31913a94b10bc773c43acc60c2f47fc933b936/proof/schnorr_protocol/W64_SchnorrProtocol.ec#L1-L9) defines the protocol roles through [`M(Syscall)`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-zk/blob/dd31913a94b10bc773c43acc60c2f47fc933b936/proof/schnorr_protocol/W64_SchnorrProtocol.ec#L1-L9). In [`W64_Zp_SchnorrCorrespondance.eca`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-zk/blob/dd31913a94b10bc773c43acc60c2f47fc933b936/proof/schnorr_protocol/W64_Zp_SchnorrCorrespondance.eca#L454-L513), [`commitment_eq`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-zk/blob/dd31913a94b10bc773c43acc60c2f47fc933b936/proof/schnorr_protocol/W64_Zp_SchnorrCorrespondance.eca#L493-L505) combines sampler and modular-exponentiation correctness; [`commitment_pr_eq`](https://github.com/dfirsov/jasmin-zk/blob/dd31913a94b10bc773c43acc60c2f47fc933b936/proof/schnorr_protocol/W64_Zp_SchnorrCorrespondance.eca#L508-L513) gives equality of probabilities after decoding. Corresponding results cover the challenge, response, and verification and support the protocol's completeness, extractability, and zero-knowledge proofs. This extends the connection from a sampling routine to a protocol model. [[4]][r4]

**KEM-IND-CCA-preserving compilation.** The August 2026 revision gives Jasmin source and assembly programs interaction-tree semantics. Random-byte requests are events interpreted by uniform sampling. Under safety, stack, and pointer conditions, [Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11292v2) proves compiler correctness; [Theorems 4.2 and 4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11292v2) preserve KEM-IND-CCA advantage and oracle-game winning probabilities. [Corollary 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.11292v2) applies the result to ML-KEM, while trusting its connection to the earlier EasyCrypt security proof. The result reaches an assembly model with specified random effects; the deployed OS provider remains outside that theorem. [[5]][r5]

## 2. Other frameworks and the Lean interfaces

**VST/FCF HMAC-DRBG.** VST verifies mbedTLS C against a functional generator model. [`mbedtls_generate_Bridge`](https://github.com/PrincetonUniversity/VST/blob/c082fe210ec501219abe52b9d0f25541d7c97e59/hmacdrbg/HMAC256_DRBG_bridge_to_FCF.v#L648-L674) connects that model to the FCF core, matching output bytes and updated state under successful-call, block-length, and no-reseed conditions, with prediction resistance disabled. [`Refactored`](https://github.com/PrincetonUniversity/VST/blob/c082fe210ec501219abe52b9d0f25541d7c97e59/hmacdrbg/HMAC256_DRBG_bridge_to_FCF.v#L680-L687) identifies the core with FCF's [`Generate`](https://github.com/PrincetonUniversity/VST/blob/c082fe210ec501219abe52b9d0f25541d7c97e59/hmacdrbg/HMAC_DRBG_nonadaptive_result.v). Probability enters through [`Instantiate`](https://github.com/PrincetonUniversity/VST/blob/c082fe210ec501219abe52b9d0f25541d7c97e59/hmacdrbg/HMAC_DRBG_nonadaptive_result.v), which samples the initial key and state uniformly. [`HMAC_DRBG_PRG_NA_true`](https://github.com/PrincetonUniversity/VST/blob/c082fe210ec501219abe52b9d0f25541d7c97e59/hmacdrbg/HMAC_DRBG_nonadaptive_result.v) proves security for nonadaptive requests. This experiment differs from NIST's actual initialisation procedure; the separate entropy-stream model includes failure but supplies no entropy-quality theorem. [[6]][r6]

**The Last Yard / SSProve (CPP 2024).** The development proves its Jasmin-to-SSProve translation correct and relates translated implementations to Hacspec specifications. Sampling occurs in wrappers around deterministic kernels: [`JOTP`](https://cpv.au.dk/pdfs/2024-cpp-last-yard-final.pdf) samples a key before calling translated XOR, and [`JPRF_real`](https://cpv.au.dk/pdfs/2024-cpp-last-yard-final.pdf#page=12) supplies a random nonce to the encryption computation. [`JOTP_OTP_perf_ind`](https://cpv.au.dk/pdfs/2024-cpp-last-yard-final.pdf#page=5) equates implementation-based and abstract one-time-pad games. For the encryption example, [`JPRF_perf_ind`](https://cpv.au.dk/pdfs/2024-cpp-last-yard-final.pdf#page=12) and [`CPRF_perf_ind`](https://cpv.au.dk/pdfs/2024-cpp-last-yard-final.pdf#page=12) connect the translated implementation to the abstract scheme, under state-separation conditions. The paper excludes while-loops from its translation and leaves probabilistic transfer to assembly outside the result. [[7]][r7]

**CryptoVerif and CV2F\*.** CryptoVerif also supports the reverse direction, from a security model to generated code. Cadé and Blanchet's [Theorem 8.38](https://bblanche.gitlabpages.inria.fr/publications/CadeBlanchetJCS14.pdf#page=81) equates attack probabilities for the model and generated OCaml-like code. Their [assumption A1](https://bblanche.gitlabpages.inria.fr/publications/CadeBlanchetJCS14.pdf#page=3) requires a perfect RNG; the translation proof is a manual semantic proof. The OCaml binding [`random="f"`](https://bblanche.gitlabpages.inria.fr/CryptoVerif/manual.pdf) associates a model type with a runtime sampler required to realise its distribution. [[8]][r8] [[9]][r9] CV2F\* instead uses the pure interface [`rand_T : entropy -> entropy * T`](https://bblanche.gitlabpages.inria.fr/publications/BlanchetFromherzetalCCS26.pdf); [`call_with_entropy`](https://bblanche.gitlabpages.inria.fr/publications/BlanchetFromherzetalCCS26.pdf) threads this abstract state through generated F\* code. CryptoVerif's security statements become F\* axioms. Their probability bounds remain in the external application theorem. The embedding is trusted. Entropy threading exposes successive RNG uses; its probabilistic interpretation comes from the model and binding contract. [[10]][r10]

**Aeneas, VCV-io, and Interop.** At the inspected upstream revision, Aeneas defines [`Result`](https://github.com/AeneasVerif/aeneas/blob/1dc8ad31b1b062a77c50e29144a6c7bba955fa09/backends/lean/Aeneas/Std/Primitives.lean#L51-L90) as [`ITree RustEffect`](https://github.com/AeneasVerif/aeneas/blob/1dc8ad31b1b062a77c50e29144a6c7bba955fa09/backends/lean/Aeneas/Std/Primitives.lean#L51-L90); the standard effect signature contains failure but no random-byte request. In VCV-io's Interop, [`toPolyFun_bind`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/cffdd70e31c18c6aaf5ff008c84b19c203fdc26d/Interop/Aeneas/ITree.lean) proves that the generic interaction-tree translation preserves sequencing, and [`toPolyFunHom`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/cffdd70e31c18c6aaf5ff008c84b19c203fdc26d/Interop/Aeneas/ITree.lean) packages it as a monad morphism. Such a map can transport an RNG event once the source signature contains one. Separately, [`liftRustM`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/cffdd70e31c18c6aaf5ff008c84b19c203fdc26d/Interop/Rust/RustM.lean#L54-L60) embeds return, failure, and divergence into [`RustOracleComp`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/cffdd70e31c18c6aaf5ff008c84b19c203fdc26d/Interop/Rust/Common.lean) without making oracle queries; [`liftRustM_bind`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/cffdd70e31c18c6aaf5ff008c84b19c203fdc26d/Interop/Rust/RustM.lean) proves compatibility with sequencing. These are structural connections between computation types. Assigning probability to an extracted RNG call still requires an identified request and its interpretation. The inspected Interop dependency is an Aeneas fork using [`RustM`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/cffdd70e31c18c6aaf5ff008c84b19c203fdc26d/Interop/lake-manifest.json), so it must be distinguished from the upstream [`Result`](https://github.com/AeneasVerif/aeneas/blob/1dc8ad31b1b062a77c50e29144a6c7bba955fa09/backends/lean/Aeneas/Std/Primitives.lean#L51-L90) revision. [[11]][r11] [[12]][r12]

**[`runIO`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/cffdd70e31c18c6aaf5ff008c84b19c203fdc26d/VCVio/OracleComp/RunIO.lean#L24-L29).** VCV-io's [`OracleComp.runIO`](https://github.com/Verified-zkEVM/VCVio/blob/cffdd70e31c18c6aaf5ff008c84b19c203fdc26d/VCVio/OracleComp/RunIO.lean#L24-L29) executes an existing `ProbComp` by answering uniform queries through Lean's [`IO.rand`](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/v4.35.0/src/Init/Data/Random.lean). It is an execution interpreter, whereas extraction and Interop concern the connection to source computations. The definition proves no equality between runtime and ideal distributions. Lean 4.35.0 uses a stateful [`StdGen`](https://github.com/leanprover/lean4/blob/v4.35.0/src/Init/Data/Random.lean), and its range conversion permits bias. Consequently, this execution path needs a separate RNG argument before it can realise the ideal semantics used in a security theorem. [[13]][r13]

## 3. Comparing the real and model games

The following separates RNG replacement from implementation correspondence. It is a general transfer argument, rather than a theorem asserted for every development above.

Let $P$ be the implementation, $M$ its specification, $R_{\mathrm{real}}$ the concrete RNG service, and $R_U$ the ideal service. Fix an implementation adversary $A$ and a translated specification adversary $A^\dagger$. The translation must be independent of the hidden bit $b$. For $b\in\{0,1\}$, define

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_b^{\mathrm{real}}&=\Pr[G_b(P,R_{\mathrm{real}},A)=1],\\
p_b^{\mathrm{ideal}}&=\Pr[G_b(P,R_U,A)=1],\\
q_b^{\mathrm{spec}}&=\Pr[G_b(M,R_U,A^\dagger)=1].
\end{aligned}
$$

These are unconditional probabilities for complete adaptive experiments, with errors and nontermination accounted for. The notation for $M$ permits internal sampling rather than a literal RNG call.

Suppose, for each $b$, that

$$
|p_b^{\mathrm{real}}-p_b^{\mathrm{ideal}}|\leq\rho_b,
\qquad
|p_b^{\mathrm{ideal}}-q_b^{\mathrm{spec}}|\leq\eta_b.
$$

The bound $\rho_b$ justifies replacing the RNG. The bound $\eta_b$ expresses implementation correspondence under ideal randomness. With the advantage convention $\operatorname{Adv}=|p_1-p_0|$, the triangle inequality gives

$$
\operatorname{Adv}_{\mathrm{real}}(A)
\leq\operatorname{Adv}_{\mathrm{spec}}(A^\dagger)
+\rho_0+\rho_1+\eta_0+\eta_1.
$$

Exact correspondence gives $\eta_0=\eta_1=0$. If both RNG comparisons are bounded by $\rho$, the additional term is at most $2\rho$. The coefficient depends on the advantage convention and the actual reduction. A trusted extraction step remains a premise supporting correspondence; an unverified translator does not acquire a numerical error bound. Concrete security also requires accounting for the time, queries, and data used by $A^\dagger$ and the RNG distinguishers.

**The OS-RNG contract.** For $R_U$, the next $n$ bytes are uniform conditional on $n$ and the complete prior experiment history, including earlier random choices and states. For $R_{\mathrm{real}}$, the comparison must cover the interaction permitted by the game: repeated requests, relevant state exposure or reseeding, and observable failures. A computational indistinguishability assumption can supply the $\rho_b$ bounds; uniform marginal outputs alone are insufficient. For Rust, [`OsRng`](https://docs.rs/rand_core/0.6.4/rand_core/struct.OsRng.html) delegates to platform services through [`getrandom`](https://docs.rs/getrandom/0.3.4/getrandom/), whose documentation assumes cryptographically secure system randomness. The source version and platform determine the concrete interface being modelled. [[14]][r14]
